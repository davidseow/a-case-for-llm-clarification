Why three words move the needle

It's an instruction. And this thing is built to follow instructions.

It isn't detecting confusion and deciding to report it. It's doing as it's told — and two separate forces make that land hard.

Trained to comply

Following an explicit instruction is the behaviour it was trained hardest to produce. Those three words spend that.

Otherwise suppressed

Right after a request, producing the goods is what gets rewarded — asking is rare. The instruction lifts that brake.

When to spend the twelve seconds

Worth it
  • Anything with a deliverable at the end
  • Anything expensive or slow to redo
  • Multi-step work that builds on step one
  • Whenever you used a vague adjective: better, cleaner, modern, tighter
Skip it
  • Quick lookups and conversions
  • Throwaway drafts you'll rewrite anyway
  • When any reasonable reading is fine by you

Rule of thumb: the more it costs to be wrong, the more the question is worth.
